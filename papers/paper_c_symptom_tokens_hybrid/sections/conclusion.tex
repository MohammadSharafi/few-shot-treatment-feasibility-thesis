\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conc}

Symptom Tokens and an ETHOS-style transformer provide a structured representation for early ICU labs and vitals, compatible with few-shot meta-learning. \textbf{Calibrated stacking} with a strong Random Forest closes most of the AUROC gap to the best tabular export (\textbf{0.746} vs.\ \textbf{0.747}) on a fixed MIMIC-IV benchmark, while na\"{\i}ve few-shot training without fusion remains much weaker in the Exp1 harness (\textbf{0.611} vs.\ \textbf{0.762}). These results argue for treating \textbf{representation design} and \textbf{fusion strategy} as first-class reporting dimensions in clinical deep learning.
