\begin{acknowledgments}
This work was inspired by my experience with the Mayo Clinic platform, where I encountered firsthand the tension between the need for intelligent health prediction and the strict constraints on data access. At the time, I could obtain only identifiers and codes---not the underlying clinical data---which highlighted how often patients and researchers face barriers to using health information for good. That experience motivated me to explore methods that can learn from distributed, privacy-sensitive data without centralization: federated learning, few-shot adaptation, and compact representations such as Symptom Tokens. I thank my supervisor, Dr. Alimohammadzadeh, for his guidance, and all those who supported this journey.
\end{acknowledgments}

\begin{abstract}
Predicting treatment feasibility from limited, aggregated clinical data is a critical challenge in personalized healthcare decision support. We address this using a novel pipeline combining \emph{Symptom Tokens}---a compact representation of labs and vitals---with the ETHOS encoder, few-shot meta-learning, and federated training. Experiments use a 2,000-patient MIMIC-IV v3.1 sample, split into five disease nodes (sepsis, cardiac, respiratory, renal, diabetes).

We establish a strong \textbf{tabular ceiling}: the SOTA pipeline (\texttt{exp\_tabular\_sota.py}) reaches \textbf{test AUROC 0.748} (F1 0.674, accuracy 0.700). The unified best few-shot pipeline (\texttt{exp\_fewshot\_best.py}) with stacking achieves \textbf{AUROC 0.746} (95\% bootstrap CI [0.699, 0.797])---\emph{matching the ceiling within sampling noise}---while the few-shot sub-model alone reaches AUROC 0.670. Under the separate Exp1 comparison script (\texttt{exp1\_main.py}), \texttt{Proposed\_FewShot} attains \textbf{AUROC 0.611} on the test split, illustrating that episodic Exp1 metrics and the stacked ``best'' pipeline are not interchangeable. Federated training costs about \textbf{0.5 percentage points} AUROC vs.\ centralized on our setup.

Key innovations include Proto-MAML-style adaptation, knowledge distillation from a Random Forest teacher, supervised contrastive pre-training, engineered tabular features, gated feature attention, cross-attention prototypes, and a stacking meta-learner fusing few-shot and classical predictions.

We contribute: (1)~a reproducible MIMIC-IV feasibility benchmark with a clear reporting hierarchy (ceiling $\rightarrow$ hybrid $\rightarrow$ stacked few-shot $\rightarrow$ federated); (2)~Symptom Tokens plus ETHOS for few-shot learning; (3)~evidence that stacking closes most of the gap to the tabular ceiling; and (4)~open code and auto-exported tables for replication.
\end{abstract}
