Table~\ref{tab:mimic_flow} summarizes the MIMIC-IV data extraction and preprocessing pipeline. We join \texttt{patients} $\to$ \texttt{admissions} $\to$ \texttt{icustays} on \texttt{subject\_id} and \texttt{hadm\_id}; \texttt{labevents} and \texttt{chartevents} are linked via \texttt{stay\_id}. The preprocessing pipeline selects 15 labs and 7 vitals, normalizes to [0,1], applies feature engineering, and produces either a 25-D tabular vector or a token sequence for ETHOS (128-D embedding). The cohort is stratified by primary ICD-10 diagnosis for federated partitioning.

Select & 15 labs + 7 vitals & ItemIDs from \texttt{d\_labitems}, \texttt{d\_items} \\

\section{SOFA Proxy and Clinical Severity}
\label{sec:sofa_proxy}
%==============================================================================

We compute a SOFA proxy from creatinine, platelets, SpO$_2$, and lactate. Higher SOFA (worse organ dysfunction) correlates with lower feasibility, as expected clinically. The mean SOFA proxy for feasible patients is 0.35 vs.\ 0.52 for infeasible ($p < 0.001$, Wilcoxon rank-sum).

\section{Class Balancing}
%==============================================================================

We explored SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling) to address the 42/58 class imbalance. For our task, class weighting in the loss function and threshold tuning on the validation set sufficed; SMOTE did not yield consistent gains. This may be because the imbalance is moderate and the decision boundary is well-separated for many samples.

\section{Missingness and Data Quality}
%==============================================================================

Clinical data often contain missing values due to irregular sampling, test ordering practices, or patient-specific factors. We analyzed missingness patterns across our cohort. Laboratory values (e.g., lactate, troponin) are more frequently missing than routine vitals (heart rate, blood pressure). For our tokenization, we use forward-fill for time-series and population median for completely missing features. Table~\ref{tab:missingness} summarizes missingness rates by feature category. Features with $>$30\% missingness (e.g., lactate in non-septic patients) may require careful handling in deployment; our min-max normalization with clipping mitigates the impact of imputation artifacts.

\section{Data Quality Assessment}
%==============================================================================

We assessed data quality using several criteria: (1) \textbf{Completeness}: Fraction of non-missing values per feature; routine labs (creatinine, sodium) have $>$90\% completeness; specialty labs (lactate, troponin) have 65--80\%. (2) \textbf{Plausibility}: Values within clinical ranges (e.g., creatinine 0.1--30 mg/dL); we clip outliers. (3) \textbf{Consistency}: No contradictory values (e.g., discharge before admission); we validated timestamps. (4) \textbf{Representativeness}: Cohort matches MIMIC-IV age and sex distribution; our stratified sampling ensures balance across disease nodes. These checks support the validity of our experimental results.

\section{Temporal Patterns}
%==============================================================================

We examined temporal trends within the first 24 hours. Patients with improving lab trajectories (e.g., decreasing creatinine, stable glucose) tend to have higher feasibility rates. The SOFA proxy, computed from peak values in the first 24h, correlates with feasibility (Section~\ref{sec:sofa_proxy}). Our tokenization aggregates temporal information into a fixed-length sequence; future work could explore explicit temporal modeling (e.g., LSTM over hourly bins) to capture trends more directly.

%==============================================================================
\section{Lab and Vital Value Distributions}
%==============================================================================

We examined the distribution of key lab and vital values across the cohort. Creatinine: median 1.1 mg/dL (IQR 0.8--1.8); 12\% of patients have values $>$2.0 (indicating renal impairment). Glucose: median 140 mg/dL (IQR 110--180); glycemic variability is high in critically ill patients. Hemoglobin: median 9.2 g/dL (IQR 7.8--10.5); 28\% have values $<$8.0 (anemia). Lactate: median 1.6 mmol/L (IQR 1.0--2.5); elevated lactate ($>$2.0) in 35\% of patients correlates with lower feasibility. These distributions inform feature engineering and clinical interpretation of model predictions. The SOFA proxy, computed from creatinine, platelets, SpO$_2$, and lactate, has median 0.42 (IQR 0.28--0.58); higher values indicate worse organ dysfunction and lower feasibility rates.

%==============================================================================
\section{Admission Type and Discharge Destination}
%==============================================================================

We analyzed feasibility by admission type (elective vs.\ emergency) and discharge destination. Emergency admissions have lower feasibility (38\%) than elective (48\%), consistent with acuity. Discharge to home: 22\% of cohort; to rehabilitation: 18\%; to skilled nursing: 15\%; to other acute care: 25\%; in-hospital death: 8\%. Our feasibility label combines home, home health, and rehabilitation as feasible; the remaining as infeasible. This binary simplification may lose nuance (e.g., skilled nursing vs.\ long-term acute care) but provides a tractable prediction target.

%==============================================================================
\section{Correlation Structure and Feature Redundancy}
%==============================================================================

The correlation structure reveals expected clinical relationships. Creatinine and BUN (blood urea nitrogen) correlate strongly (r $>$ 0.7)---both reflect kidney function. Hemoglobin and hematocrit are highly correlated (r $>$ 0.9)---redundant for prediction. Sodium and chloride correlate (r $>$ 0.6)---electrolyte balance. These correlations inform feature selection: removing redundant features (e.g., hematocrit if hemoglobin is present) could simplify the model without losing predictive power.

\section{Chapter Summary}
%==============================================================================

This chapter presented comprehensive data analysis of the MIMIC-IV cohort: database schema and extraction pipeline, cohort demographics and disease node distribution, lab and vital correlations, SOFA proxy validation, age-stratified performance, error analysis, embedding analysis, class balancing, missingness patterns, data quality assessment, temporal trends, feature distributions, and correlation structure. Key findings include: (1) the cohort is balanced across disease nodes; (2) lab correlations reflect known clinical relationships; (3) the SOFA proxy correlates with feasibility; (4) classification errors concentrate at borderline lab values; (5) ETHOS embeddings show partial class separation (silhouette score 0.12). These insights inform the model architecture, feature engineering, and interpretation of results presented in subsequent chapters.
